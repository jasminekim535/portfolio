NOW INSTITUTE

Website Design for Morphosis


During my internship at Morphosis Architects, I independently designed and launched the official website for the Now Institute, an urban research initiative focused on sustainable city planning, climate resilience, and infrastructure equity.

I also built a bilingual (Japanese/English) site for Noto Now, a Now Institute project outlining an urban recovery framework for Japan's Noto Peninsula after the 2024 earthquake, drawing from a publication and ongoing work by USC architecture graduate students.

I consolidated content from outdated, scattered sources into modern, responsive sites for a professional and government-facing audience — building the site architecture, updating project content, and establishing a simple design system using Figma, GitHub, Vercel, and Claude Code.

●OVERVIEW

Website Design
There was a need to translate researched information into intuitive, cohesive websites for the institution.

I designed two websites: one serving as the overarching site for the Now Institute itself, showcasing its projects and publications.

The other was dedicated specifically to Noto Now, built to raise awareness about the community in Noto, Japan, and its recovery after the 2024 earthquake.




●PURPOSE & FUNCTION

Problem & Solution
The Now Institute needed a complete digital overhaul due to outdated content, broken pages, and poor UX across two disconnected websites. I rebuilt the platform from the ground up, improving layout and structure to better communicate the institute's impact and credibility.

Noto Now required a seperate website, translating dense recovery research into an accessible experience that raises awareness about the community's post-earthquake recovery.

●PROCESS

Wireframing, Prototyping, Iterating
1. Created designs on Figma, benchmarking similar institutions and experimenting with Figma Make.

2. I connected Claude Code to the Figma MCP workflow to generate and manage the website code through GitHub and deployed it using Vercel.

3. Through continuous iterations, I refined the design, layout, typography, and copy while updating project pages and reorganizing content to improve the overall user experience, responsiveness, and visual consistency of the site.
●DESIGN THINKING

Now Institute: I focused on improving UX through layout and navigation, researching all projects to synthesize their scattered, unkept content into one clear identity. I built a design system with a geometric sans-serif typeface, black-and-white palette, structured layouts, translucency, and hover interactions.
Noto Now: I focused on narrative storytelling — site structure, page orientation, and information architecture — along with the Japanese/English balance and color palette. A serif typeface and upscale aesthetic reflected the earthquake's gravity, paired with imagery celebrating the region's cultural heritage, to raise awareness and encourage action from government and public audiences.

●DESIGN

Noto Now: Designing Noto Now started with empathy — understanding how to honor loss while highlighting resilience. I shaped a narrative structure guiding users from the earthquake's impact toward the region's cultural strengths and rebuilding vision. I iterated on page flow, bilingual balance, and visual tone, testing whether a serif typeface and upscale aesthetic conveyed gravity without overshadowing Wajima's warmth — building a site meant to inform and move government and public audiences to action.


Now Institute: Designing the Now Institute site started with the goal to improve the user experience of navigating scattered, disconnected work. I researched every project to synthesize that content into one clear identity, improving layout and navigation along the way. I built a design system — geometric sans-serif type, black-and-white palette, structured layouts, translucency, and hover interactions — and iterated until the site felt unified rather than assembled.
