A 52-card deck, risograph-printed in layers: each suit has its own ink, the sheets were printed several times for even color, then cut down to card size.

Process

  1. 01DesignEvery card in black and white
  2. 02SeparateOne ink per suit
  3. 03PrintLayer by layer, multiple sheets
  4. 04CutTrimmed to card size

●Design

Why it’s black and white

Risograph prints one ink at a time, so each suit was drawn as a black-and-white layer. Black marks where its ink lands; the color only appears on press.

Cute eyes, every card

Each card shares one detail: small eyes with their own expression, so the whole deck feels like one family.

A twist on the numbers

Pips are arranged into playful shapes instead of the standard grid, so every number reads differently.

Imagery per suit

Each suit gets its own motifs, from umbrellas and cakes to fish and clouds, built from the suit symbol.

●Risograph

Four suits, four inks

Each suit was printed in its own ink color. I ran multiple sheets of every layer to keep the color even across the deck, then cut the sheets into cards.

●Final print

Improved designs and reprint

After feedback from my class and professor, I refined the designs and reprinted the full deck, with a patterned back.
