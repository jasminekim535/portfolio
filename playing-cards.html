Playing Cards

RISO Printmaking Playing Card Design

TIME
December 2025
TOOLS
Playing Card Design
CREDIT
Instructed by Vin Caponigro
SMFA 2025 Fall
This project explores the balance between structure and expression by redesigning a traditional 52-card playing deck. Working within the fixed conventions of standard playing cards—format, hierarchy, and usability—I created a unique visual system for each card inspired by its suit, shape, and numerical value. Abstract forms, eyes, and repeating patterns were used to introduce variation while maintaining cohesion across the deck. Through multiple print iterations, the project evolved from initial experimentation to a refined physical object, incorporating material, color, and production choices that enhance both function and visual impact.





●Experimenting with Card Design

Creating My Playing Cards
I designed a full 52-card deck, creating a distinct design for each card while preserving the traditional structure and usability of standard playing cards. Using the shapes and numerical logic of each card as a foundation, I developed a visual system built from eyes, abstract forms, and repeating patterns. The Aces were designed to feel bold and iconic, while the Jack, Queen, and King share a unified composition to reinforce hierarchy and cohesion across the deck.


●Process

Initial Print  
The first print focused on translating the designs into a physical format at standard playing card size. I used a retro-style paperweight paper, which resulted in cards that felt too thin, with outlines remaining and unrounded corners. The deck also lacked a card back, limiting both its functionality and completeness. Each suit was assigned a simple primary-based color palette—red, teal, purple, and blue—establishing an early system for visual consistency.


●Final Prints

Improved Designs and Reprint
After receiving feedback from my class and professor, I reprinted the deck with several key improvements. I selected a sturdier cardstock paper, removed the outlines, and added rounded corners to reflect the feel of traditional playing cards better. I also refined the designs and added a backing with a flat-gold polka-dot pattern to unify the deck and enhance its finish. The original color system and standard playing card dimensions were maintained, ensuring continuity while elevating the cards' overall quality and usability.
