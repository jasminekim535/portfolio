TRANSFORMATION OF KOREA

Research Exhibition Poster Design 


In collaboration with the exhibition at Fletcher’s 10th Conference on Gender and International Affairs, ‘Liberation and Resilience in Uncertain Times’, I researched my personal connection to the topic of gender and international affairs. Raised in South Korea, I have researched its history and grown up in a country that has developed immensely in a remarkably short period. Incorporating South Korea’s Transformation in my graphic design poster, I have experimented with design elements and composition. 

South Korea achieved liberation on August 15, 1945, when Japan surrendered at the end of the Pacific War. Three years later, on August 15, 1948, the Republic of Korea (ROK) was formally established as a democratic nation. However, peace was short-lived; on June 25, 1950, North Korean forces, equipped with Soviet-made tanks and aircraft, invaded the South, igniting the Korean War. The brutal conflict, which lasted until the signing of an armistice on July 27, 1953, devastated the Korean Peninsula. Millions of soldiers and civilians lost their lives, industrial facilities were obliterated, and South Korea was left among the poorest nations in the world.

In the decades that followed, however, South Korea achieved a remarkable transformation. Emerging from an impoverished country, it has become a high-income nation renowned for its advanced technology, robust education system, and innovative industries. Today, South Korea’s cultural influence extends globally, largely thanks to the widespread popularity of K-pop, K-dramas and films, Korean cuisine, K-beauty, and other aspects of its vibrant culture.

Despite being a relatively small country, South Korea has had a significant impact on increasing Asian representation in the global entertainment industry. K-pop, a genre that faced initial criticism for its unconventional styles and use of makeup and fashion among male idols, has evolved into a global phenomenon. Once viewed as "non-masculine" by Western standards, K-pop now boasts an immense and devoted fan base worldwide. Through this globalization of culture, South Korea has not only showcased its own identity but also fostered greater awareness and acceptance of cultural diversity worldwide.
